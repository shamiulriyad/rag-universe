# Why RAG?

> **Status:** Planned (Phase 2) · **Level:** Beginner
>
> This page is a placeholder. It lists the planned scope so contributors and learners know what is coming. The lesson content has not been written yet.

## Planned Learning Objectives

After this lesson, you will be able to:

- Describe the limitations of LLMs that RAG addresses: knowledge cutoff, no access to private data, hallucination, and lack of source attribution.
- Explain why RAG reduces but does **not** eliminate hallucination.
- Identify situations where RAG is a good fit and situations where it is not.

## Planned Outline

1. What an LLM knows, and what it cannot know.
2. Problem 1: knowledge cutoff and stale information.
3. Problem 2: private and domain-specific data.
4. Problem 3: hallucination and unverifiable answers.
5. How retrieval helps with each problem, and where it falls short.
6. When **not** to use RAG.
7. Summary and exercises.

## Planned References

- Lewis, P. et al. (2020). *Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks.* [arXiv:2005.11401](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401)
- Liu, N. F. et al. (2023). *Lost in the Middle: How Language Models Use Long Contexts.* [arXiv:2307.03172](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172)
